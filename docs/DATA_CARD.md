## How the data is used

1. **Demand and supply geography** — real origin/destination state shares replace the hand-made weights.
2. **Real order bootstrap** — a 20,000-order sample (joint origin, destination, month, weight, volume, value, category, promise) feeds model training, the Digital Twin and demo replay.
3. **Route physics** — per-route real carrier-transit medians scale every simulated carrier's transit time; per-route late-rate relative to the network sets route risk; real zip-to-zip distances replace centroid distances.
4. **Seasonality** — observed monthly lateness above the network mean (this includes Nov-2017 *and* the Feb–Mar 2018 disruption).
5. **Tariffs** — simulated carrier prices are scaled per destination region so their median equals observed Olist freight for comparable orders.
6. **Business impact** — review-score penalty for late delivery.
7. **Benchmark** — carrier-agnostic ETA and late-risk models trained on real orders and scored on a chronological hold-out.

## Limits (read before quoting numbers)

- Olist publishes **no carrier identifier**. Carrier-level behaviour (prices, speeds, reliability, capacity) remains simulated behind the adapter contract; the real data constrains the *routes* and *market* those carriers operate in.
- The customer promise in Olist data is heavily padded, which is why the late rate is low. The benchmark therefore measures predictability of marketplace outcomes, not carrier quality.
- Feb–Mar 2018 is a network-wide disruption period, not a stable seasonal pattern; treat its month penalties as stress scenarios.
- Data ends Oct 2018; nothing here is live.
